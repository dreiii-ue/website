Why Do We Accept This Kind of Transportation and Should We Still?

    I use my motorcycle every day to get through Philippine traffic, not because I want to, but because I feel like I have no better option. Each ride reminds me how broken our transportation system is, from endless congestion to roads that feel unsafe even in daylight. As I go through traffic, I often ask myself why commuting here has to feel like a daily struggle instead of a normal part of life. I can’t help but compare our situation to countries like Singapore and Hong Kong, where transportation is efficient, reliable, and designed for people. It makes me question why Filipinos are forced to adapt to a failing system instead of benefiting from one that actually works.

    Riding a motorcycle has become a survival tool rather than a lifestyle choice, especially when public transportation is overcrowded, unreliable, and poorly planned. I see more students and workers choosing motorcycles or private cars because commuting wastes too much time and energy, which only makes traffic worse. The problem goes beyond vehicles—it’s deeply rooted in poor infrastructure like uneven roads, unclear lane markings, sudden roadworks, and designs that ignore motorcycle riders altogether. When I look at Singapore or Hong Kong, their systems show what’s possible when government and private companies work together with long-term planning. If the Philippines redesigned its transportation system to prioritize efficiency, safety, and accessibility, more people would willingly commute instead of adding to the congestion.

    I believe that fixing transportation could dramatically improve the daily lives of students and workers across the country. Less time stuck in traffic means more time for rest, productivity, and mental well-being. A better system would reduce the need for risky choices like riding motorcycles through dangerous roads just to arrive on time. While the challenges feel overwhelming, change is still possible with proper planning, accountability, and cooperation between the public and private sectors. If the Philippines redesigned its transportation system to prioritize efficiency, safety, and accessibility, more people would willingly commute instead of adding to the congestion.
